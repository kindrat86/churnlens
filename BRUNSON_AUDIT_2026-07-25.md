# Brunson Secrets Trilogy Audit — churnlens.site

**Date:** 2026-07-25 · **Auditor voice:** Russell Brunson (first person) · **Prior audit:** 2026-07-18 (claimed composite 65.4)

---

## The one-paragraph version

Your funnel machinery is *better built than 95% of what I see* — you have a value ladder, a squeeze page, an OTO, a stack, an epiphany bridge, a hero's-two-journeys section, a manifesto, a Dream 100, an affiliate page, and two Perfect Webinar scripts. And almost none of it was working. Your five-day follow-up sequence had **never sent an email to anyone** because opt-ins were never added to the list the sender reads. Your two Perfect Webinar assets had **zero inbound links** from anywhere on the site. Your OTO — the highest-intent moment in the whole funnel — asked for a $49/mo commitment through a `mailto:` link, while the one thing a customer could actually buy sat two clicks away on the pricing page. And the proof holding it all up was invented: a testimonial from a customer you have never had, a "$340K on 11 of 14 deals" statistic that contradicted your own "15 deals" statistic, and a $7,000 value stack built from numbers nobody ever charged. I fixed all of it. **The honest composite went from 49 to 55**, and the reason it is 55 and not 75 is Traffic — which is now your only real problem.

## What I found that was not true, and fixed

Every item below was live on the domain this morning.

1. **A fabricated customer testimonial** on `/oto` — *"I ran the 5-Risk analysis on a $680K deal… negotiated $87K off the price. The $9 report saved me 9,666x. — Verified ChurnLens user."* You have no customers. This is the most serious thing on the list; it is invented proof attributed to a person who does not exist.
2. **"11 of 14 deals"** on `/masterclass` versus **"15 deals"** on the homepage and `/walkthrough` — two mutually inconsistent versions of the same private anecdote, both presented as product proof. Your own homepage carries a code comment from commit `41f0834` saying the 4.2× figure must never be reintroduced; it had survived in a belief card and in the `/about` stat pills.
3. **False delivery speed in five places** — "60 seconds", "3-minute report", "report in 2 minutes", "Instant report delivery" — against a real turnaround of 2 business days, human-reviewed.
4. **A countdown timer that enforced nothing.** `/oto` ran a 10:00 "this offer expires when you leave" clock over a public Stripe link.
5. **Invented value anchors** — "$97/mo value" line items, "Total: $342", "Total: $341", "$7,000/audit". No one has ever been charged any of these.
6. **"Full SOC 2 audit pending Q4 2026"** on `/pricing`. No audit is engaged.
7. **Features sold that do not exist** — API access, deal-flow tracking, unlimited uploads, on a product that is a human-reviewed manual service. Marked as roadmap or corrected.
8. **The synthetic sample report called "real"** on 21 pages and in the checklist email, while the report itself is stamped SYNTHETIC throughout.
9. **Affiliate infrastructure that does not exist** — a 90-day cookie, a signup portal, a payout schedule.
10. **A `from $99/mo` Stripe link** on `/7-revenue-churn-red-flags` for a price published nowhere else on the site.
11. **A static "Next benchmark report update: 6 days"** that never counted down.

## What to do next, in order

I did not touch these four, because each needs a decision only you can make.

1. **Record `/masterclass` as a YouTube video.** The script is finished. This is the highest-return hour available to you and it fixes your worst chapter.
2. **Spend $200 on paid.** Not to scale — to learn. You have never bought a click, so you have no idea what your CPL is, and Brunson's whole "buy your way in" argument is that you cannot optimise what you have never measured. Retarget `/dream100` Tier 1 audiences.
3. **Execute ten Dream 100 touches this week** and log them. The page is a beautiful plan that has never been run. Ten emails to Tier 4 (DD consultants, SaaS M&A advisors) is a week's work and they are affiliate-ready by construction.
4. **Decide on a high-ticket rung.** Your ladder stops at $199 and Brunson's whole ladder argument is that the top rung funds the bottom one. A "full deal review, by application" at $1,500–$2,500 is the obvious shape — but I will not publish a price or a capacity promise you have not agreed to.

One more, cheaper than all of them: **state your Big Domino in one sentence** and put it at the top of `/masterclass`. You have been circling it across 800 pages without ever saying it.
